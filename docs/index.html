Day 18: Confirming the Limit Cycle Is a Global Attractor and the Bifurcation Is Supercritical

Two experiments today. First: five perturbation magnitudes spanning three orders of magnitude are applied to the mature stock; all converge to the same oscillatory orbit by t = 550, confirming the limit cycle is a stable global attractor. Second: a forward-backward sweep through resource_decrease with diffusion switched off tests for hysteresis. The two curves are indistinguishable within numerical error and the amplitude grows continuously from zero at the transition, confirming the bifurcation is supercritical Hopf.

Pushing effort far beyond realistic levels reveals a second bifurcation: the limit cycle dies at around effort ≈ 8, via damped oscillations rather than a sudden collapse. Day 16: The Unstable Fixed Point, the Limit-Cycle Advantage, and What Fishing Does to It

Predictor-corrector integrator shows limit-cycle biomass is ~1.23× the unstable fixed point at λ=2.0 (vs ~1.94× from Euler); bifurcation occurs at λ≈2.08, with the advantage ranging from ~1.28 at λ=1.925 to zero at λ≥2.1. The limit cycle is a cohort wave (2.5× recruits, 0.5× juveniles, 1.5× large adults), persists under all tested fishing effort (0–0.5), and mean biomass drops only 6% while yield scales linearly with no collapse threshold.
